# One Lucky Dog — Tier 1 Competitor Watchlist (v1)

*Working document for the in-house competitive intelligence pipeline. This is the baseline monitoring set: the competitors whose moves most directly threaten, validate, or reshape OLD's positioning. Treat every entry as editable — tiering is a judgment call, not a fact, and should be revisited quarterly.*

---

## How to read this document

Each entry carries the fields the pipeline needs to ingest it:

- **Role / segment** — where they sit relative to OLD's three pillars (options, crypto, prediction) and why they're Tier 1.
- **Public / private** — this determines the data pipeline. **Public** companies give you free, structured, high-signal financials and roadmap via SEC EDGAR (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, proxy) and earnings-call transcripts — this is the layer you'd otherwise rent from AlphaSense. **Private** companies require the product-surface + press + funding-announcement approach.
- **Threat vector to OLD** — the specific way they pressure our differentiation. This is what the impact rubric scores against.
- **Primary surfaces to wire** — ranked by signal density for that company. (App-store release notes and pricing/fee pages are the highest-signal, lowest-noise surfaces for almost everyone; press/blog is what they *say*, filings and release notes are what they *do*.)

## The convergence thesis (read this first)

Your original framing treated options, crypto, and prediction as three separate watchlists. As of 2026 that's the wrong mental model. The category is consolidating into **multi-asset active-trader platforms**, and the consolidation is happening through acquisition, not organic build:

- **Coinbase → Deribit** (Aug 2025): crypto exchange buys the #1 crypto *options* venue.
- **Kraken → NinjaTrader** ($1.5B): crypto exchange buys a *futures/multi-asset* brokerage.
- **Robinhood → MIAXdx** (closing Q1 2026): retail broker buys its own *prediction-market exchange + clearinghouse*, after prediction markets became its fastest-growing revenue line.
- **Kalshi → crypto perps**: prediction-market venue pushes into *crypto derivatives*.

**Implication for OLD:** your "options-native core + crypto + prediction under one roof" is not a novel wedge on the *products* — the giants are assembling the same footprint. Your defensible wedge has to be the *content/education flywheel, the anti-guru positioning, the income-as-architecture product design, and transparent active-trader pricing*. The watchlist should therefore weight **how competitors are packaging and positioning** these products (and to whom) as heavily as which products they ship. A "convergence tracker" that flags each time a competitor adds one of the other two pillars is a standing high-value signal.

## TIER 1 — OPTIONS / ACTIVE-TRADER CORE (OLD's home turf)

### 1. tastytrade (tastylive)
- **Role:** The single closest analog and your structural precedent — and a **founder-lineage platform**: built by OLD's own founders, now run by IG Group. Options-native brokerage fused to a content engine, with the entity-separation model you're replicating. Watching them is watching what your founders' prior venture has become under new ownership — where it's drifted, what it's kept, and which of their original bets IG has (or hasn't) pursued.
- **Public / private:** Private (owned by IG Group). No SEC filings — but **IG Group is publicly listed in the UK (LSE: IGG)**, so IG's annual/half-year reports and investor presentations disclose tastytrade metrics, strategy, and US ambitions. This is your back-door into their financials. *(Note: your CEO's IG background is a live knowledge asset here.)*
- **Threat vector to OLD:** They already own the content-to-brokerage flywheel and the active-options-trader audience you're courting. Any move into crypto/prediction, any pricing change, any new educational format, or any shift in the tastylive/tastytrade content boundary is directly instructive.
- **Surfaces to wire (ranked):** iOS + Android release notes → tastylive YouTube (upload cadence, topic drift, new show formats) → pricing/commission page → IG Group investor relations & LSE filings → careers page → platform changelog.

### 2. Robinhood
- **Role:** The scale aggressor spanning **all three** of your pillars — options, crypto, and prediction (via Kalshi, now building its own exchange). The mainstream-audience threat.
- **Public / private:** **Public (NASDAQ: HOOD).** Full EDGAR + earnings transcripts. They are unusually explicit about roadmap and per-product traction on calls — prediction-markets contract volumes were disclosed there first.
- **Threat vector to OLD:** Distribution scale, brand, and a demonstrated willingness to move fast into every category you occupy. Their prediction-market economics (fastest-growing revenue line) validate that pillar for you *and* signal a well-capitalized incumbent racing to own it. Watch their move from Kalshi-partner to owning MIAXdx — vertical integration of the exchange layer.
- **Surfaces to wire (ranked):** SEC filings + earnings calls (roadmap gold) → newsroom/blog → iOS + Android release notes → pricing/fee pages → careers (esp. derivatives/prediction/futures roles).

### 3. Webull
- **Role:** Mobile-first active-trader platform, charting-forward, options + crypto. Overlaps your active-trader UX ambitions and mobile-first launch.
- **Public / private:** **Public (NASDAQ: BULL, via 2025 SPAC).** EDGAR + transcripts now available — newer filer, so history is thin but growing.
- **Threat vector to OLD:** Direct competition for the mobile active-options trader on UX, charting depth, and promotional pricing. A benchmark for "table stakes" mobile experience.
- **Surfaces to wire (ranked):** iOS + Android release notes (very active shipper) → pricing/fee page → SEC filings → blog/newsroom → careers.

### 4. Interactive Brokers (IBKR)
- **Role:** The professional/serious active-trader benchmark. Deep options, added crypto, and runs its **own** event-contract market (**ForecastEx**) — i.e., already tri-pillar.
- **Public / private:** **Public (NASDAQ: IBKR).** Rich filings + transcripts; also publishes monthly brokerage metrics (DARTs, accounts) — a free, high-frequency traction signal most competitors don't disclose.
- **Threat vector to OLD:** Sets the ceiling on tooling depth, order routing, and breadth for the sophisticated trader. Their ForecastEx is a reference implementation for how a broker builds prediction markets in-house rather than partnering.
- **Surfaces to wire (ranked):** SEC filings + monthly metrics → ForecastEx announcements → platform release notes/changelog → pricing → careers.

### 5. Public.com
- **Role:** The positioning overlap you should watch most closely. "Transparent," rebate-disclosing, multi-asset (options, crypto, bonds) with a content/community lean — it competes for the *same "transparent active-trader"* narrative space OLD is claiming.
- **Public / private:** Private (VC-backed). No filings — monitor via product surfaces, press, and funding announcements.
- **Threat vector to OLD:** Direct competition for your *positioning wedge*, not just your products. If they lean harder into transparency and education, they blunt your differentiation. Watch their messaging as closely as their features.
- **Surfaces to wire (ranked):** Blog/newsroom + positioning language → iOS + Android release notes → pricing/transparency pages → social → careers → funding news (Crunchbase/press).

### 6. Moomoo (Futu)
- **Role:** Charting- and data-heavy active-trader platform, options + crypto, aggressive on mobile and on IPO-access hooks.
- **Public / private:** Parent **Futu Holdings is public (NASDAQ: FUTU).** Filings cover the group; US-specific (Moomoo) detail is partial — supplement with product surfaces.
- **Threat vector to OLD:** Analytics/charting depth and feature velocity aimed at the data-driven trader; a benchmark for in-app analytics richness.
- **Surfaces to wire (ranked):** iOS + Android release notes → in-app feature/analytics announcements → Futu SEC filings → pricing → blog.

## TIER 1 — CRYPTO

### 7. Coinbase
- **Role:** The US crypto benchmark, and now — post-Deribit — the **most comprehensive crypto derivatives/options platform globally.** The convergence archetype coming *from* crypto *into* options.
- **Public / private:** **Public (NASDAQ: COIN).** Deep filings, detailed shareholder letters and quarterly "in review" posts that read like roadmap documents.
- **Threat vector to OLD:** As Deribit integration reaches US customers (pending CFTC DCM/DCO licensing), Coinbase becomes a crypto-*options* competitor with enormous distribution. Watch the US regulatory/licensing progress closely — that's the gate on when they become a direct threat in your options wheelhouse.
- **Surfaces to wire (ranked):** Shareholder letters + SEC filings → Coinbase blog + Deribit Insights → US derivatives licensing/regulatory news → app release notes → pricing → careers.

### 8. Kraken
- **Role:** Crypto exchange executing the clearest **crypto-into-multi-asset-brokerage** convergence play via the NinjaTrader acquisition (futures, and a path into broader brokerage).
- **Public / private:** Private but **pre-IPO** — confidentially filed a draft S-1 (Nov 2025) and publicly signaling readiness. Expect a public S-1 (a massive one-time intelligence document) and then ongoing filings. Until then: product surfaces + funding/press.
- **Threat vector to OLD:** NinjaTrader gives them a real active-trader/futures brokerage rail; the combination points at exactly OLD's cross-asset footprint from the crypto side. The eventual S-1 will be a rich competitive read — flag it as a priority ingest when it drops.
- **Surfaces to wire (ranked):** NinjaTrader product + integration announcements → Kraken blog → S-1 / IPO filings (when public) → app release notes → pricing → funding/press.

## TIER 1 — PREDICTION MARKETS

### 9. Kalshi
- **Role:** The CFTC-regulated prediction-market leader and the rails behind several competitors' event-contract offerings. Now expanding **into crypto perpetual futures** — converging toward your footprint from the prediction side.
- **Public / private:** Private (well-funded, ~$2B+ valuation range). No filings — but as a **CFTC-regulated DCM**, its regulatory footprint, product certifications, and contract listings are partially public through CFTC channels. That regulatory surface is a real, free signal source.
- **Threat vector to OLD:** Owns the prediction-market category's mind-share and regulatory head start; its crypto-perps move makes it a cross-asset competitor, not just a prediction venue. Its contract-listing cadence is a live read on where retail event-trading demand is heading.
- **Surfaces to wire (ranked):** New contract/market listings (product cadence) → CFTC filings/certifications → Kalshi blog + social → app release notes → partnership announcements (who's white-labeling their rails).

### 10. Polymarket
- **Role:** The crypto-native prediction-market heavyweight, now operating in the US again and backed by **ICE/NYSE** — bridging crypto-native prediction into regulated US rails.
- **Public / private:** Private, **pre-IPO** (raising at ~$12B; ICE strategic investment; native token planned). No filings yet; monitor product + funding + regulatory surfaces.
- **Threat vector to OLD:** Sets the pace on prediction-market product innovation (e.g., new contract categories like private-company markets) and, via the ICE tie, on the legitimization/institutionalization of the category. A leading indicator of where prediction-market UX and market types are going.
- **Surfaces to wire (ranked):** New market-category launches → Polymarket blog + social → ICE/NYSE partnership + token news → funding/valuation news → app/web release notes.

## Platform benchmark & convergence watch

These sit alongside the core ten but on different cadences and for different reasons.

### Platform benchmark (quarterly deep-audit, not monthly monitoring)

**Charles Schwab / thinkorswim** *(Public: NYSE: SCHW)* — the *gold-standard* options platform and the UX/feature bar for the serious options trader. It's here for a specific reason: like tastytrade, thinkorswim is a **founder-lineage platform** — built by OLD's own founders, now run by Schwab. It is the standard *they personally set* for what an options platform should be. Auditing it quarterly answers two questions at once: (a) what's the current table-stakes bar for serious options UX, and (b) how has the founders' original creation evolved — or stagnated — under incumbent ownership. Not a nimble monthly threat; a benchmark to measure against deliberately, a few times a year.

### Convergence watch (monitored for pillar-additions, not full profile)

**Crypto.com** — aggressively expanding across crypto derivatives, prediction markets, and equities. Not aimed squarely at the US active-options trader today, so it doesn't warrant a full Tier 1 monitoring profile. But it's a textbook convergence player, so it feeds the **always-on convergence alert** (below): the trigger is "Crypto.com adds/expands a pillar aimed at US active traders," at which point it's promoted to full Tier 1.

## Pipeline design implications (ties to your in-house / low-cost goal)

Since you're building the earnings/filings layer in-house rather than renting AlphaSense, the public/private split above maps cleanly onto two ingestion paths:

**Path A — Public competitors (COIN, HOOD, IBKR, BULL/Webull, FUTU/Moomoo, SCHW/thinkorswim; IG Group for tastytrade).**
Primary source is **SEC EDGAR**, which is free and structured: the submissions API and full-text search give you 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, and proxy filings programmatically, and earnings-call transcripts are obtainable and highly extractable. This is where you build the "financial data + earnings insights" layer yourself. High signal, low cost, low maintenance (EDGAR doesn't break like a scraper). IBKR's monthly brokerage metrics and Coinbase/Robinhood shareholder letters are especially roadmap-rich. IG Group's LSE filings are your public proxy for tastytrade.

**Path B — Private competitors (tastytrade-direct, Public.com, Kalshi, Polymarket, Kraken-until-IPO, Crypto.com).**
No filings, so lean on product surfaces (app-store release notes, pricing pages, changelogs), press/funding announcements, and — for the CFTC-regulated ones (Kalshi, Polymarket) — regulatory filings and contract certifications, which are a genuinely underused free signal. Flag Kraken's and Polymarket's eventual S-1s as priority one-time ingests.

**One cross-cutting note:** app-store release notes and pricing/fee pages are the highest-value, lowest-maintenance surfaces across *both* paths. If you build only two collectors first, build those two. Everything else is enrichment.

## Resolved decisions (v1 locked)

1. **Baseline size:** Ten core Tier 1 names, ~4–6 surfaces each. Locked.
2. **thinkorswim/Schwab:** Included as a **platform benchmark** on a quarterly deep-audit cadence (not monthly). Founder-lineage rationale documented above.
3. **Crypto.com:** **Convergence watch** — feeds the always-on convergence alert, promoted to full Tier 1 if it pushes into US active-options trading. **Gemini:** dropped from active monitoring; parked in Tier 2.
4. **Convergence alert:** A **dedicated, always-on alert** — a first-class output, separate from the monthly digest. Trigger: any watched competitor (including convergence-watch names) adds, acquires, or materially expands one of the other two pillars. This is the standing signal the whole thesis turns on.
5. **Geographic scope:** **US-only.** Non-US launches are out of scope — even where features are interesting, the US regulatory environment is usually the gating blocker, so foreign product velocity is a weak predictor of what actually reaches your market.
6. **tastytrade weighting:** **Standard weight**, same as other Tier 1 names — the founders' direct, current knowledge of tastytrade makes a heavier automated monitoring profile redundant.

### Standing outputs (what the pipeline produces)

- **Monthly digest** — feature/pricing/roadmap changes across the ten Tier 1 names, scored by the impact rubric, with a "so what for OLD" line each.
- **Always-on convergence alert** — fires on any pillar-addition move (decision 4).
- **Quarterly benchmark audit** — deep read of thinkorswim as the platform-standard reference.
- **Priority one-time ingests** — Kraken S-1, Polymarket S-1, and Coinbase's US Deribit licensing milestone, flagged for immediate deep-read when they land.
